## Lesson objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to make intentional decisions about fade height, build and remove guidelines cleanly, adapt around the client’s head shape, and inspect the finished fade under lighting that exposes the work instead of flattering it.

## Fade height is a design decision

Low, mid, and high are not just labels on a menu.

Where you place a skin fade changes the shape of the whole haircut. It can make the head read longer, rounder, narrower, heavier, or lighter depending on where the transition sits and what you leave above it.

That is why you choose fade height according to the client in front of you, not according to whatever fade you happen to like doing most.

This lesson covers the zero-degree skin fade, low skin fade, mid skin fade, high skin fade, and skin fade with a side part.

## Build guidelines you can actually remove

A guideline is useful only if it gives you structure without creating a problem you cannot clean out later.

Work deliberately. Use the lever and half-guard control your instructor demonstrates. Prepare with trimmer or foil where appropriate. Keep checking whether the transition is actually moving or whether you are just making the same area shorter.

Do not chase darkness by pushing the fade higher.

That is one of the fastest ways to lose the shape.

## The head is not a perfect surface

Real heads have dents, protrusions, uneven density, recession, scars, and growth patterns that change how light hits the fade.

You have to blend the head that is there, not the diagram in your head.

Pay special attention to the parietal ridge. Removing too much weight there can turn a controlled fade into a hollow shape very quickly.

When you think the fade is finished, change the lighting and the angle. Strong light will show you what comfortable shop lighting can hide.

## Instructor standard

Charlie or the supervising barber will demonstrate the approved fade sequence, foil or trimmer preparation, lever work, and half-guard approach. The written lesson gives you the checkpoints; the exact technical sequence is learned under supervision.

## Product focus: definition versus movement

Use Clay Pomade when the shorter top needs matte control, stronger definition, and volume.

Use Texturizing Paste when the haircut should keep more movement and a more natural finish.

The choice should come from the haircut and the client’s routine, not from habit.

## Client-building application: follow up

Within 24 hours, follow up with every model.

Keep it simple: thank them for letting you work with them, ask how the haircut is settling in, invite honest feedback, and offer the next maintenance appointment.

The follow-up is not just customer service. It gives you information about how your work wears after the client leaves your chair.

## Workbook handoff

Use Lesson 2.4 to document the reason for your fade-height choice, where you lost time, which areas required the most correction, the follow-up response, and what you would change on the next service.
