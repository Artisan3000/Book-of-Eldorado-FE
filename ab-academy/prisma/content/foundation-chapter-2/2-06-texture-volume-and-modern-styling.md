## Lesson objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to create texture and volume without removing the density the haircut needs, use drying direction as part of the shape, and choose styling products according to movement, hold, and finish.

## Texture is not random removal

Texture should solve something.

Maybe you need separation. Maybe you need movement. Maybe the haircut is carrying too much bulk. Maybe the fringe needs to sit differently. Maybe a curly top needs to be evaluated one curl at a time instead of treated like straight hair.

What texture is not: taking weight out everywhere because the haircut feels heavy in one place.

That is how holes happen.

## Know what you are protecting

This lesson covers textured tops, front French crops, textured pompadours, curly-cut tops, and classic beach-cut tops.

You will work with point cutting, channel cutting, controlled texturizing, fringe control, curl-by-curl visual evaluation, and deliberate blow-dryer direction.

In fine hair, preserving density may be more important than creating more visible texture. In thicker hair, reducing bulk may be necessary, but the goal is still shape and movement, not thinness for its own sake.

Every removal should have a reason.

## The blow-dryer is a shaping tool

Modern styling does not start when the finishing product comes out.

Direction, lift, tension, and airflow change how the haircut reads.

If you want volume, create it deliberately. If you want movement, dry for movement. If the client wants something relaxed, do not build a finish that feels frozen in place.

The goal is volume without stiffness and texture without chaos.

## Instructor standard

Charlie or the supervising barber will demonstrate the approved use of point cutting, channel cutting, bulk removal, curl evaluation, and fringe control for each model. These techniques require judgment; they are not interchangeable shortcuts.

## Product focus: build the finish in layers

40 Proof Sea Salt Spray gives light-to-medium hold, texture, and added volume. Apply it evenly from the roots on damp or towel-dried hair when you need a textured pre-styling base.

Texturizing Paste gives flexible hold, a natural finish, separation, and movement.

Advanced Volumizing Foam adds fullness with flexible hold without aiming for a heavy or crunchy finish.

Think of these as different tools for building the same haircut in different ways. Product should help reveal the cut, not compensate for a shape that was never built correctly.

## Client-building application: document the work

Prepare three portfolio posts from approved services.

Each one should include a clear before-and-after, the correct haircut name, one sentence about the client’s goal, the product used, and a direct booking instruction.

Only use client photographs when you have clear permission.

A portfolio is not just proof that you can cut. It teaches future clients what to ask you for.
