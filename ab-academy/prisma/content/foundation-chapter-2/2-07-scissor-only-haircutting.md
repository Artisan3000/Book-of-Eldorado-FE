## Lesson objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to build a scissor-only shape with clean sectioning, intentional guides, controlled elevation and overdirection, and enough movement that the haircut still feels natural when the client styles it themselves.

## Scissor-only does not mean less structure

When the clipper disappears, your shape has to come from sectioning, guides, elevation, overdirection, graduation, layering, and perimeter control.

That gives you more freedom, but it also gives you more ways to lose the plan.

This lesson covers short, medium, and long all-scissor cuts, all-scissor top blends, and textured scissor-cut sides.

The common goal is controlled length, natural movement, and a softer silhouette.

## Know where your guide is

A stationary guide and a traveling guide do different jobs. Elevation and overdirection change where weight lives. Graduation and layering change how the shape builds and moves.

These are not vocabulary terms to memorize for a quiz. They are decisions you need to see in the finished haircut.

If you lose the guide, stop and recover it. Do not keep cutting and hope the shape finds itself.

## Work with growth, recession, and movement

Longer hair makes growth patterns more visible, not less.

Cowlicks, recession, density shifts, and natural movement all affect where a perimeter should sit and how much weight a section can support.

Dry detailing matters here because the haircut changes when the hair falls naturally.

The final check should happen in the shape the client is actually going to wear.

## Instructor standard

The supervising instructor will demonstrate the approved sectioning, guide, elevation, overdirection, graduation, layering, and dry-detailing approach for each service. The written lesson is there to keep the decisions clear while the hands-on standard is demonstrated in person.

## Product focus: support movement

30 Proof Styling Cream works when the client needs light hold, natural sheen, and soft control.

Texturizing Paste adds flexible movement and separation.

40 Proof Sea Salt Spray can be used as a pre-styler when the haircut needs volume and texture before the finish.

Everyday Conditioner supports lightweight hydration and manageability. After shampooing, concentrate it through the mid-lengths and ends and rinse after two to three minutes.

Longer hair often needs less product than people think. Build control without burying the movement you just spent the service creating.

## Client-building application: recommend maintenance based on shape

Do not give every client the same return date.

A short scissor cut, a medium layered shape, and a longer cut will not lose their structure on the same schedule.

Explain when you expect the haircut to stop behaving the way it was designed to behave. That is the maintenance recommendation.

## Practical assignment

- Complete three supervised scissor-only models at different lengths.
- For each model, record the guide strategy, elevation/overdirection decisions, and maintenance recommendation.
- Use the Client & Service Tracker and Apprentice Scorecard after each service.
