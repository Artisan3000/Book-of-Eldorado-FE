## Lesson objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to adapt your service for a younger client without sacrificing safety or professionalism, communicate with both the child and parent, and approach advanced elective looks only after the foundational work is under control.

## Comfort comes before speed

A children’s haircut asks for a different kind of control.

The client may move. They may be nervous about the tools. Their attention span may be shorter. The parent may have one expectation while the child has another.

Your first job is not to force the appointment to behave like an adult service.

Your first job is to create a safe, patient experience.

## Talk to both people in front of you

Speak to the child directly. Explain the tool before you use it. Let them know what is happening next.

At the same time, keep the parent informed about the haircut plan, length, finish, and any product you intend to use.

Work with movement instead of getting frustrated by it. Avoid unnecessary pressure around the hairline. If the child is no longer comfortable enough to continue safely, know when to stop and reschedule.

Finishing faster is never more important than finishing safely.

## Supervision is required

Children’s services in this stage are completed only with Charlie, Cathy, or another designated senior professional supervising and approving the work.

That is not optional. The goal is to build judgment in a setting where the client’s comfort and safety remain protected.

## Advanced elective work comes after the foundation

This lesson also opens the door to elective looks such as a FoHawk back taper, a classic or clipper-blended mullet, or a skin-faded mullet.

These are not shortcuts around the foundational work you have already been doing.

They come after you have demonstrated control of the core cuts.

Creative work is more useful when the fundamentals underneath it are stable.

## Product focus: keep it light

Use little or no product on a child unless the parent wants styling.

When a soft, natural finish makes sense, a light application of Styling Cream may be appropriate.

Confirm product use with the parent before applying it.

## Client-building application: family relationships

If the child and parent had a good experience, you can naturally mention future family appointments.

Keep it low-pressure. Let the experience do most of the selling.

A simple close is enough: you are glad the child was comfortable, you would be happy to reserve the next haircut, and you also work with adult clients through the Artisan apprenticeship program.

## Practical assignment

- Complete two approved children’s services under designated supervision.
- Complete one advanced elective model after receiving instructor approval.
- Document comfort/safety observations, parent communication, product approval, service outcome, and instructor feedback in the workbook.

## Workbook handoff

Use Lesson 2.9 to reflect on how you adapted the service, record any moments where comfort changed your technical plan, and document the instructor approval for the advanced elective work.
