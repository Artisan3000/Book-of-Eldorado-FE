## Lesson objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to bring the entire chapter together in live services: lead the consultation, make the technical plan, execute the haircut or beard service, choose and explain the finishing product, document the result, and turn good work into the beginning of a returning clientele.

## The capstone is about decisions

You have spent this chapter practicing individual families of cuts, product choices, service habits, documentation, and client follow-up.

Now those pieces have to work together without the lesson telling you which decision comes next.

That is the point of the capstone.

You will complete two services:

- One clipper-and-scissor or scissor-only haircut.
- One taper, skin fade, beard combination, or textured haircut.

The client chooses the desired result. You lead the consultation, explain the plan, complete the service, choose the finishing product, give home-care instructions, photograph the result with permission, and offer the future appointment.

The instructor is evaluating the whole service, not just the cleanest detail in the finished cut.

## Use the Apprentice Scorecard

Your capstone uses the same scorecard you have been building against throughout the chapter.

- Consultation and understanding of the client’s goal — 15 points.
- Sectioning, tool control, and safe technique — 15 points.
- Shape, proportion, and silhouette — 20 points.
- Blend, detailing, hairline, and finish — 20 points.
- Product selection and application — 10 points.
- Professionalism, cleanliness, and client comfort — 10 points.
- Timing, home-care explanation, and rebooking — 10 points.

The total is 100 points. A passing score is 80% or higher.

A serious sanitation, client-safety, or professional-conduct problem is still a fail even if the numerical score is above 80%. The standard is not just technical.

## Product examination

You will also be given five sample client situations.

For each one, you need to identify the desired hold, the desired finish, whether the product belongs on damp or dry hair, the proper amount, the best product for the situation, and how you would explain home use to the client.

This is not about memorizing labels.

It is about showing that you can connect a client need to a product decision and explain that decision clearly.

## Build the next 30 days

The chapter does not end when the capstone is over.

You are also building a 30-day booking plan.

Include the models who may return, clients who are due for maintenance, referral prospects, three portfolio posts per week, five personal outreach messages per week, your available appointment times, and the dates when you need to follow up.

This is where practice starts turning into a book.

A strong month is not something you wait for. You give yourself enough follow-up, enough visibility, and enough reasons for people to come back that a real pattern can start forming.

## Graduation requirements

To complete Chapter 2, you need:

- Two passing capstone services.
- A product examination score of at least 80%.
- A portfolio of 15 approved looks.
- 30 documented supervised services.
- A starter list of at least 12 returning or rebooking clients.

## What this chapter was really building

The haircut matters. Of course it does.

But the standard you have been practicing is bigger than a single haircut.

You are learning to see the client clearly, make a plan before you move, use the right tool for the right reason, explain your choices, finish with intention, document the work, and create a reason for the client to come back.

That is how technique turns into a professional practice.

You do not graduate from this chapter because you never need supervision again. You graduate because you have enough structure to keep getting sharper on purpose.
